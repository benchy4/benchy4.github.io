Defend: the trailing team either hits the tying three (→ overtime coin-flip), draws a four-point play on a closeout foul (→ near-certain loss), or misses (→ near-certain win).
Foul: two free throws produce four possible outcomes. The two "one make, one miss" outcomes leave the final free throw live for an offensive rebound scramble, which the trailing team occasionally converts.

Defend: their held possession ends in a score (split between 2s and 3s) or a stop, then it's your last possession — needing a 2 to win, a 2 to tie, or a 3 to tie, depending on the resulting deficit and however much clock survived their hold.
Foul: two free throws produce three outcomes (miss both / split / make both), each leaving you a possession with the leftover clock after the whistle.

Sources this model is built from
  • Zapata-Hall (2024), "College Basketball: An In-Depth Study of the 'Foul Up 3' Dilemma" — 13 seasons of NCAA D1 play-by-play (58,000+ games), overtime-adjusted win% by time/shot-clock cell. Found fouling recommended in more situations than conventional wisdom suggests, especially with a fresher shot clock, and estimated a ~1.6 percentage-point average win-rate gain from following the optimal cell-by-cell strategy.
  • McFarlane (2019), "Evaluating NBA End-of-Game Decision-Making," Journal of Sports Analytics — an NBA-specific win-probability model (the "End-of-game Tactics Metric"). Found intentional fouling up 3 becomes optimal around 9 seconds remaining, and that trailing teams should generally start fouling earlier than they typically do, with the optimal window opening earlier as the deficit grows — directional support for the trailing-team tree above, though that tree is our own explicit branch model rather than a fit to his reported numbers.
  • Kenter (Rice University), "An Analysis of the Basketball Endgame: When to Foul When Trailing and Leading" — models the endgame as a combinatorial game and concludes both trailing and leading teams should foul earlier and more often than they do in practice; a case-study team was estimated to have cost itself ~9.6 expected wins in a season through suboptimal fouling.
  • Ken Pomeroy, "Yet Another Study About Fouling When Up 3" (2013) and "The Guide to Fouling When Leading (or Tied)" (2020) — found teams shooting to tie in these exact situations converted only ~16% of threes, and that offensive-rebound rates on the resulting missed free throw run well above normal (roughly 10–15% at baseline, spiking toward 25%+ when every offensive player crashes the boards).
  • inpredictable, "The Hackulator" (2019) — a win-probability-based fouling calculator for trailing teams; documents that real NBA teams foul less often than the model recommends.
  • "Thinking Basketball" clutch-shooting data — leaguewide 3-point rate rises from ~17% of shots to ~27% in clutch time, while 3P% falls from ~38% to ~31%, a meaningfully worse look than the raw clutch average.
  • Cao, Price & Stone (2011, University of Oregon) — found free-throw shooting drops roughly 5–10 percentage points below season average in the final moments of close games.
